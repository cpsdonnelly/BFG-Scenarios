M36 ~ M42 REDEMP-
TION ERA

As the Imperium enters a new era, one
it hopes will be brighter than the dark
times it has endured, the facts never-
theless serve as a stark reminder of a sad
truth: the Golden Age is now a thing of
the past. After five millennia of existence
and trials, the Imperium has irrevoca-
bly lost the knowledge that once made it
great.

Anew fleet

As the Age of Apostasy drew to a close,
the relentless civil wars that had ravaged
the Sector since Nova Terra took their
terrible toll: the Battlefleet Hellenos had
been decimated, and apart from a few
rare ships that had managed to survive
those times, nothing remained of the
earlier eras. But now, even the Geor's
Tryptic no longer had the power to resur-
rect those ships of yore, and the shipyards
had lost their ancient expertise for good

This decline affected the entire Impe-
rium, but until then, the Battlefleet
Hellenos had been an exception. Thanks
to the Adeptus Mechanicus, the old
classes continued to be built, although
the quality gradually declined. Thus, it
took the Sector Imperial Navy almost a
millennium to abandon its ancient ves-
sels in favour of the new generation em-
bodied by the Lunar-class cruisers; with
these, the doctrines of old vanished, and
finally, the Battlefleet Hellenos moder-
nised to adopt the new Imperial stan-
dard. Following a long path of gradual
evolution, the Murder, which first ap-
peared in M33, thus gave way to the Lu-
narat the dawn of M37.

THE NAVIGATOR
CRUSADE

By a twist of fate, by the end of the 38th
millennium, the Hellenos Sector found
itself in a particularly prosperous po-
sition. Having miraculously escaped
large-scale invasions for centuries, the
Industrial Worlds reached a peak in pro-
duction, to the extent that the Sector au-
thorities had to find a place to spend this
surplus. At the same time, Warp routes
to the north of the Sector were disco-
vered, and the Geor's Tryptic, in a rare
moment of unity, demanded new ter-
ritories. Thus, the Sector Governor de-
clared the Crusade of his own accord, wi-
thout the approval of the Adeptus Terra.

Suddenly, the lives of the hundreds of
billions of humans in the Hellenos Sec-
tor were focused on a single goal: to ma-
nufacture, fight and conquer in the name
of humanity's glorious right to conquer
the entire Galaxy. To support this effort, a
vast shipbuilding project was launched,
and Roditene was, of course, called upon
to operate at full capacity.

200 hundreds year plan

Faced with this truly extraordinary bud-
get, the Imperial Navy devised a ship-
building programme spread over 200
years. The plan was intended not only to
completely renew the Battlefleet Helle-
nos but also to create the new armada
that would defend the newly conquered
Sector. The scale of the task was titanic,
but if there was ever a time when this
challenge stood a chance of being met, it
was surely this day.

The most significant part of this plan
was undoubtedly the construction of the
battleships. The plan indeed called for
the rapid construction of four of them
in just two centuries. Among them were
two Emperor-class Battleships and two
Indarcus-class Battleships, an ancient
model designed by the Forge-World of
Roditéne. Although already impressive,
this programme also included a range of
Cruisers of all types. Among these, the
main innovation was the design of the
Pursuit Cruisers. These were intended to
improve the versatility of standard Impe-

tial Cruisers, granting them the speed to
pursue pirates and the firepower to des-
troy them swiftly. This led to the Aris-
tocrat, Dignitary and Oligarch classes,
which were ultimately intended to com-
pletely replace standard Imperial Crui-
sers, starting with the Dominator class.
Finally, Light Cruisers and Escorts roun-
ded out the fleet, the only innovation
in this area being the Hurricane Heavy
Frigates, a heavy and cumbersome class
designed to escort Capital Ships.

Bitter epitaph

Despite the relative success of the Navi-
gators’ Crusade, this era came to a sad
end for the Battlefleet Hellenos. After
two centuries of war, the veterans of
the Crusade split between the Hellenos
Sector and the Hecate Sector, the latter
of which received, as promised, a subs-
tantial fleet. But the overall outcome
remained mixed: the Pursuit Cruisers,
built in large numbers, revealed all their
flaws; the shipbuilding programme had
been hampered by delays and failures;
and the losses reduced the numerical
advantage with which the Imperial Navy
had hoped to emerge from the conflict.
Nevertheless, the end of the Crusade was
marked by the launch of four Battleships
from the Roditene shipyards, an event
eagerly awaited by all the Sector's Admi-
rals, followed by the conversion of the
Ladon’s Breath into a vessel equivalent to
the Nemesis class.
