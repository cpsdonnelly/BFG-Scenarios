DESPOT CLASS

The Despot class is the latest addition
to the Pursuit Cruiser line; it was first
introduced in the first century of M39
following the emergence of factions fa-
vouring attack craft. As such, the Despot
is based almost entirely on the Dicta-
tor-class cruisers. However, centuries of
experience with Pursuit Cruisers provi-
ded important lessons, preventing the
mistakes of the past from being repeated.
Thus, whilst the Despot carries the Dic-
tator’s weapons and the Geor macro-can-
non turrets of the Pursuit Cruisers, it was
designed to be larger in order to accom-
modate extended ammunition stores.

History

During the first century of M39, long af-
ter the introduction of the first Pursuit
Cruisers into the Battlefleet Hellenos in
M37 and the building boom that charac-
terised that era, a new school of thought
was gaining influence. Indeed, a number
of officers were beginning to favour the
use of attack craft over other types of ves-
sel. But this faction remained too weak
to influence shipbuilding decisions on
its own. However, they found allies in the
Pursuit Cruiser lobby as well as other mi-
nor influential groups who shared a com-
‘mon hatred of the ruling orthodoxy. This
unlikely alliance gave rise to the Despot
class, but it quickly fell apart, with the
result that only a handful of these ships
were built. Nevertheless, the few in ser-
vice proved excellent on patrol. In reality,
they were simply better-armed and faster
Dictators, which explains the success of
this class.

DIGNITARY CLASS

The Dignitary class is a Gothic upgraded
to the standards of the Pursuit Cruiser.
Specifically, it features three additional
Geor macro-cannon turrets and im-
proved thrusters. Unlike other Pursuit
Cruisers, Dignitarys never suffered from
ammunition shortages for the simple
reason that the Gothic’s laser lances were
replaced by ammunition magazines.
However, the Dignitary lacks the power
system required to operate long-range
laser lances, meaning that getting within
range can prove more difficult than for
other Pursuit Cruisers.

History

During the Navigators’ Crusade, the Fu-
rious Virtue came up against a Devas-
tation-class cruiser. Accelerating at full
speed towards its opponent to unleash
all its Laser Lances, but the Devastation’s
bombers struck the Furious Virtue first.
Unfortunately, the few Macro-cannons
failed to destroy the approaching wave,
and the anti-aircraft fire barely scratched
the formation. The poor Imperial ship
was devastated and reduced to a drif-
ting wreck, taking its entire crew with
it. Although the ship was subsequently
salvaged and repaired, this terrible event
prompted the Imperial Navy to exercise
the utmost caution when facing enemy
aircraft.

OLIGARCH CLASS

The Oligarch perfectly epitomises the
shortcomings encountered by the Pur-
suit Cruisers. Armed entirely with
macro-cannons, ammunition shortages
reach their peak with this class. Shortages
in combat became so severe that the car-
go holds were converted into additional
ammunition stores, even going so far as.
to draw down fuel reserves - a move that
could prove disastrous if a shot were to
hit the ammunition. Consequently, the
Oligarch has a reduced range in order
to be able to see a battle through to the
end. This class therefore proved to be a
failure.

HURRICANE CLASS

The Hurricane Heavy Frigates date
back to the Navigators’ Crusade and the
extensive shipbuilding programme that
accompanied it. These are heavy escort
vessels designed to protect Imperial
Cruisers from enemy attack craft. Conse-
quently, their design makes them far
more durable than other escort vessels,
as they must sail alongside the battle line,
which bears the brunt of enemy fire. In
addition, they are equipped with highly
advanced targeting systems that enable
them not only to protect allied Cruisers
with their very long-range anti-aircraft
fire, but also to more effectively destroy
enemy aircraft with their main arma-
ment. They are therefore highly effective
Escorts that carry out their mission effi-
ciently and faithfully.
